# Individual Insights: Strategy Development Process

> Module 6 · Leverage AI Tools for Leadership Execution
>
> Your personal reflection on building the strategy end to end.

## Friction points

_What was the hardest part of building this strategy, and how did you work through it?_

> The hardest part was not writing the strategy. It was deciding what to do when the pressure-test broke it in Module 1.
>
> My whole cascade rested on one assumption: that foremen don't use Meridian because it's too much effort. The AI came back with a competing explanation I hadn't considered — that the field avoids the system of record because it's an instrument of accountability against them, and the group text is chosen precisely because it's deniable. Same observed data, completely different strategy.
>
> What I did instead of patching the cascade and carrying on was put the assumption on the board as an explicit evidence gate: two weeks, three active projects, a written verdict by week 3, running in parallel with the Now bets rather than blocking them. That decision then propagated into everything downstream — it's what made me kill offline-first in M2 even though I'd scored it Impact 4, it's what my team charter protects in M3, and it's the load-bearing assumption in my M5 business case.
>
> The friction was that carrying an unresolved assumption through five deliverables is uncomfortable. It's much more satisfying to write a strategy that sounds certain. The work was in keeping it honest instead.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

> **A hard no that doesn't cost you anything isn't a hard no.** My first one — freezing analytics and ERP work for four quarters — sounded brave until the CSO prompt pointed out it cut my own differentiator. The real one was refusing the push toward the top 10 GCs, because it sacrificed revenue and made someone senior angry. That's the test.
>
> **Your OKRs can all go green while the strategy fails.** Two of my three KRs were outputs in outcome clothing: activation and feature usage, both of which a PM can simply mandate. Nothing I was measuring would have detected the group text still running the job site. I now check every KR against the question "could this hit target while the objective fails?"
>
> **A single headline number is a weakness, not a strength.** My business case assigned 100% of a $250k renewal to a form change. The CFO stress-test didn't argue with the number — it asked how much of the renewal was actually attributable. Restating it in bands at 100%, 50% and 25% lost me the 4.2x headline and gave me a case I can defend in the room.
>
> **I negotiate from position, not interest, unless I force myself not to.** In the M4 role-play I opened with "four engineers for ten weeks" and only got to the real reason — that offline-first is architectural and costs several times more to retrofit — in Round 3. The moment I said that, the conversation moved. Five minutes of preparation would have saved twenty.

## "Aha!" moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

> I had built a solution for the problem I preferred to solve.
>
> Frictionless capture is a product problem. It's the one I know how to fix, it's measurable, and it fits a roadmap. Exposure is not a product problem — it's about what a filed record costs the person who files it, and there's no feature that fixes it. So without noticing, I'd chosen the explanation that made my expertise relevant.
>
> What made it land is that it wasn't only about Meridian. In Module 3 I diagnosed a real situation with two of my own developers and reached the same shape of error from the other direction: I was reading their irritability as behaviour to give feedback on, when the honest diagnosis was that the system around them had no cycle for recognition and I hadn't moved on it for three months. Both times, the comfortable reading put the problem somewhere other than my own decisions.
>
> That's the habit I'm taking out of this: before committing to a direction, ask which explanation I'd find most convenient to be true — and then check that one first.
