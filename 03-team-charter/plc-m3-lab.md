## Name the situation
- **Who they are (role, not name), what you have observed, and how long it has been happening.:** Two fullstack developers on my team, both with significant tenure on their projects. Their delivery quality and output have stayed high throughout. Over the last three months I've observed a consistent pattern: when scope or priorities change mid-sprint, both react with visible frustration (pushback in planning, questioning why the change was made and a noticeably flatter tone afterwards). This did not happen a year ago with comparable changes. Outside of those moments they remain engaged and productive. Neither has raised compensation, role, or career progression with me directly.

## Make a diagnosis
- **Your diagnosis, plus one sentence on why. Is your frustration with their behavior, or with a decision you made?:** The behaviour I'm seeing isn't a performance or expectations problem, because their output has never dropped. What's missing is a structural one: we have no predictable cycle for reviewing conditions and progression for senior people, so recognition only happens when someone forces the conversation. Two developers who are performing at the top of the team have no visible path or timeline, and the irritability around mid-flight changes is the symptom of people absorbing cost with nothing on the other side of it.

My frustration is with a decision the company has made, or better said, it is not making, not with their behaviour. I've known for three months that they deserve better conditions and the company hasn't moved on it, and I've let the absence of a system stand in for a decision.

## Write your opening line
- **One next action I will take in the next two weeks is:** In the next two weeks I will close the design of the incentives system for the two of them (individual objectives, the collective component, and the review cadence) and take a concrete compensation proposal to the CEO with a decision date of early January. Then I sit down with each of them, show them the mechanism and the date, and tell them exactly what I've asked for and what I can't yet guarantee.
- **The first sentence of the conversation I need to have is:** I want to start with something I should have said three months ago: the way you've both handled the constant re-prioritising this year hasn't gone unnoticed, and the fact that nothing has changed in your conditions is a decision I own. Here's what I'm putting in place and the date I'll have an answer.

## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** We own the field record: everything between a foreman making an observation on site and that observation existing as a structured, compliance-ready object in Meridian.
- **What is out of scope.:** Analytics, reporting and exec dashboards (Enterprise team), ERP and Procore integration work beyond the two named client breakages, Time tracking, subcontractor portal, and any surface serving a user who is not a superintendent, foreman, or their direct field manager, Pricing and packaging of field seats (Revenue). We define who the user is; we do not define what they cost.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** Any change to a shared object (RFI, daily log, change order) that alters how the office consumes it; Any commitment of field engineering capacity to a renewal, a customer escalation, or an enterprise feature, regardless of duration; Any new notification or write path into an enterprise-owned surface; Any field surface exposed to a non-field role.
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** PM 1 decides within field adoption and mobile. PM 2 decides within enterprise. Both unconstrained up to the boundary of a shared object.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** Shared objects: joint call, in writing, within 48h. No agreement → escalate. It never defaults to whoever has a customer waiting.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** CPO within 5 working days, both positions written beforehand
